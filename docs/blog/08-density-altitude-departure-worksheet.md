Original ZuluNiner training worksheet. Companion article: https://zuluniner.com/blog/density-altitude-takeoff-climb-performance/

If converting an assumed climb rate to a ground-referenced gradient, use groundspeed and consistent units. For feet per nautical mile: climb rate in ft/min divided by groundspeed in kt/60. This arithmetic alone does not model an entire departure.

## Reference-copy exercise from the article

These are fictional plans using the 1978 Cessna 172N handbook reference copy, Figure 5-4, at 1,829 m (6,000 ft) pressure altitude. They do not represent an inspected airplane or an actual flight.

* A: 862 kg (1,900 lb), 10°C; approximately 2,085 m (6,840 ft) density altitude. Published ground roll: 267 m (875 ft); distance over 15.2 m (50 ft): 480 m (1,575 ft).
* B: 1,043 kg (2,300 lb), 30°C; approximately 2,816 m (9,240 ft) density altitude. Published ground roll: 483 m (1,585 ft); distance over 15.2 m (50 ft): 882 m (2,895 ft).

Both lookups use the table's pressure altitude and actual temperature, with its flaps-up, short-field, paved/level/dry, zero-wind conditions and all applicable notes. The reference copy does not establish the current manual revision for any aircraft.
