# Buying an older SR22 G2: what to check beyond the glass panel

Two SR22 G2s with similar-looking panels can leave you with different training needs, repair options and maintenance commitments. Compare the installed systems and the records that support them before deciding which airplane fits your flying. The generation label is a starting point for the search.

A bright display can't answer whether an advertised navigator is WAAS-equipped, what work a parachute invoice covers, or how much load the airplane can carry after its modifications. Those answers come from different documents. Keep them separate until the records, equipment and inspection agree.

This US-first guide uses sources and ZuluNiner listing records checked on **17 October 2026**. The two aircraft comparisons below describe **seller-advertised configurations**, not inspected aircraft. No service-center review or aircraft-specific maintenance schedule has been obtained; no maintenance interval, repair quotation or condition verdict is assigned here.

## Start with serial number and installed configuration

Ask for the aircraft serial number, registration and current equipment list before building a comparison spreadsheet. Add the engine and propeller models and serial numbers. Then identify the applicable Pilot's Operating Handbook (POH), supplements, weight-and-balance report and modification records. A year and a panel photograph don't establish the whole configuration.

Cirrus's [SR technical-publications catalog](https://store.cirrusaircraft.com/sr-series-online-technical-publications-suite/5637342579.p) describes a suite covering multiple SR20, SR22 and SR22T configurations, including maintenance manuals, CAPS component manuals, service information, POHs and supplements. Its [current technical-publications portal](https://techpubs.cirrusaircraft.com/) requires sign-in. A generic downloaded handbook shouldn't stand in for checking the documents applicable to the airplane you're considering.

Make the equipment list precise enough to distinguish a display from a navigator, and both from an autopilot. A primary flight display (PFD) presents flight information; a multifunction display (MFD) can present maps and other information. The units supplying navigation guidance and controlling the airplane still need their own identification.

If an ad says “turbo,” add the engine installation, modification approval and operating supplement to the request. If it says “TKS,” request the exact ice-protection installation and its approved operating limitations. Don't infer an operating approval from a system's brand name or the appearance of wing panels.

## Compare two advertised G2 panels

The following two active ZuluNiner listings illustrate the questions. They are snapshots of supplied advertisements checked on 17 October, and may change or sell. Their logbooks, installed equipment and claimed condition haven't been independently verified for this article.

| Advertised item | A: [2005 SR22 G2, N823PV](/aircraft/2005-cirrus-sr22-g2-261316121-controller) | B: [2004 SR22 G2, N701SG](/aircraft/2004-cirrus-sr22-g2-261431083-controller) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Aircraft serial number | 1383 | 0963 |
| Flight displays | Avidyne Entegra PFD and EX5000 MFD | Avidyne Entegra PFD and EX5000 MFD |
| Navigators | Dual Garmin GNS 430; the equipment text doesn't specify W | Dual Garmin GNS 430W, described as WAAS |
| Autopilot | S-TEC 55X with altitude preselect | Avidyne DFC90 |
| Engine-time wording | 1,585 hours since new | 275 hours since new, with a separate 4,205-hour airframe figure |

The displays' shared names don't erase the navigator and autopilot differences. Equally, A's missing W in the text doesn't prove that the installed units are non-WAAS. Ask for unit identification and the applicable records before categorizing it. An ad's omission is an unanswered question.

**WAAS**, the Wide Area Augmentation System, is relevant to navigation capability. [Garmin identifies the GNS 430W as a WAAS navigator](https://www.garmin.com/en-CA/p/301/pn/010-00412-01/) capable of LPV approaches, which provide lateral guidance and a vertical glidepath. That product capability doesn't establish that a particular aircraft installation is approved, correctly configured and working.

[Avidyne lists the DFC90 for approved SR20/22 configurations](https://www.avidyne.com/shop/general-aviation/dfc90-digital-autopilot/), including Entegra installations. Its [Vantage upgrade information](https://www.avidyne.com/vantage12/) also identifies replacing a legacy S-TEC 55X as an upgrade path. These are manufacturer descriptions of systems and upgrade options, not evidence that either listed aircraft needs an upgrade or meets every installation requirement.

Use the comparison to write different inspection questions. For A, establish the navigator versions and demonstrate the installed 55X's approved functions. For B, establish the DFC90 installation and its interfaces. For both, identify the guidance source, display indications and operating limitations. Avoid assigning every advertised capability to the airplane merely because a compatible product exists.

## Ask what happens when a navigator fails

Repair support belongs in a purchase comparison even when the radios work during a demonstration. Garmin's [Service Advisory 23018 Revision B](https://support.garmin.com/en-US/?faq=HLCXhipr3t4FtlEhJFFYv5) states that display-module replacement repairs for WAAS and non-WAAS GNS 430 units have ended. It describes other repair support as dependent on available components and says database updates and technical support continue.

That is a specific repair limitation. It doesn't mean every GNS 430 is unserviceable, and buying a 430W doesn't remove that display-module limitation. Ask an avionics shop what it can support for the actual units and what a replacement installation would entail if a repair isn't available.

Also avoid budgeting on the assumption that a non-WAAS 430 can simply receive the old factory WAAS upgrade. Garmin's [Service Advisory 2027](https://support.garmin.com/en-GB/aviation/faq/QlJZ7Vgdje9g6ldPtIJgx6/) ended that upgrade program in 2020. A dated product page describing an upgrade shouldn't be your purchasing plan.

Get a written scope for any proposed replacement: equipment, installation, interfaces, approval documents, weight-and-balance changes and downtime. Keep that prospective work separate from defects found in the prebuy. A personal preference for a newer navigator isn't evidence that the seller's existing installation is defective.

## Inspect the interfaces behind the screens

A menu page lighting up establishes less than an integrated-system check. Agree with the inspecting shop and appropriately qualified pilot which approved functions they will verify, how they will record the result, and which checks require a flight. Use the installation's manuals and limitations to define the work.

For an instrument-flying mission, the written scope might ask them to verify navigation-source selection, course indications, the installed autopilot's approved approach functions, disconnects and relevant alerts. Ask them to distinguish a navigator's displayed guidance from guidance the autopilot can actually follow. Don't turn this buyer's request into an improvised flight-test procedure.

An [older COPA discussion about buying a 2004 G2](https://forum.cirruspilots.org/t/about-to-pull-trigger-on-2004-sr22-need-advice/77563) raises exactly this difficulty: readers debate advertised equipment, upgrades and whether the photographs support the claims. The thread explains the audience question; its replies don't verify the equipment in today's listings.

Record subscriptions separately. Ask which navigation databases, charts, weather services and other paid functions are included, transferable or due for renewal. A demonstration performed using the seller's accounts doesn't establish what will work under your ownership.

## Turn CAPS paperwork into an itemized status sheet

**CAPS** means Cirrus Airframe Parachute System. “Fresh CAPS” or a future repack date in an ad should lead you to the underlying entries. Ask what assembly the work covered, which components were installed, the release-to-service entry and the document used to establish the next applicable action.

Have the Cirrus-experienced maintainer reconcile those entries with current, applicable manufacturer instructions and any applicable Airworthiness Directives (ADs). The publication suite includes CAPS component maintenance manuals; the account-gated manuals and an aircraft-specific service-center review weren't available for this guide. A universal interval or maintenance-price claim would exceed the evidence obtained.

| Status-sheet field | What to request |
| --- | --- |
| Aircraft and component identity | Aircraft serial number; applicable CAPS assembly/component part and serial numbers |
| Work completed | Date, aircraft time, exact task, installed parts and signed maintenance entry |
| Basis for next action | Applicable document and revision, task reference, trigger and next due status |
| Open questions | Missing records, unresolved applicability and the person responsible for checking them |

Keep each applicable item on its own row. One invoice doesn't answer whether every relevant task is current. If the seller supplies a due-date summary, ask the inspecting maintainer to trace it back to the entries and instructions rather than copying the summary into your budget.

The [FAA's AD applicability guidance](https://www.faa.gov/aircraft/air_cert/continued_operation/ad/app_comp) says searches must cover the aircraft, engine, propeller and installed appliances, with the relevant model or series. A search for “SR22” alone doesn't establish the compliance status of every installed product. Request a dated applicability and compliance review, including recurring actions where relevant.

## Read engine history on its own timeline

The two ads separate engine history from airframe history in different ways. An engine's hours since new aren't the aircraft's total time, and an overhaul is a different event again. Request the installation entry, engine identity, overhaul or new-engine documents as applicable, and the dates behind the hour figures.

Ask the inspecting mechanic to explain what the history supports and what inspection is needed. Include recorded repairs, periods of limited operation, preservation records where available, and the history behind replaced cylinders or accessories. Don't turn a small hour count into a remaining-life prediction.

The [engine-history guide](/blog/low-engine-hours-old-overhaul) explains why calendar history and the scope of prior work need separate consideration. For a candidate SR22, obtain the applicable engine manufacturer's current instructions and an assessment tied to that engine. This article supplies no engine overhaul interval and no forecast of how long either advertised engine will last.

## Recalculate the load for your mission

The ads quote different allowances for fuel, occupants and baggage: A advertises **493 kg (1,087 lb)**, and B **455 kg (1,004 lb)**, rounded here from the supplied figures. These are advertised numbers to reconcile against current weight-and-balance documents, not verified loading authorizations.

Consider a **hypothetical screening exercise**: your occupants and baggage total **300 kg (661 lb)** and your assumed departure fuel weighs **160 kg (353 lb)**. The combined load is **460 kg (1,014 lb)**. The fuel assumption isn't a route calculation or a statement that reserves are adequate.

On those rounded advertised allowances, A leaves about **33 kg (73 lb)** and B exceeds its allowance by about **5 kg (11 lb)**. This only compares total mass. It doesn't establish the permitted loading envelope, baggage limits, fuel requirements or takeoff performance for either aircraft.

Request a worked loading case using your actual occupants, their seating positions, baggage and planned fuel. Check the airplane's current empty-weight and balance data after modifications. The [four-seat mission guide](/blog/four-seats-full-fuel-aircraft-payload) walks through that process, while the [density-altitude guide](/blog/density-altitude-takeoff-climb-performance) shows why a loading result still needs a departure-performance assessment.

## Commission an inspection with named outputs

Before authorizing the prebuy, agree what you will receive. A written scope can request a records discrepancy list, applicable maintenance status, physical inspection findings, an avionics function report and a list of work requiring further investigation. Choose a shop with experience in the actual configuration, including modifications.

Ask specifically how the inspection will address composite repairs and any recorded damage. Request the repair documents and supporting inspection history rather than interpreting paint condition as a structural assessment. The buyer needs an explanation of the evidence and unresolved work, not a general reassurance based on photographs.

The [annual-versus-prebuy guide](/blog/prebuy-vs-annual-inspection) explains why the scope matters. If a current annual entry is part of the sales pitch, still establish what your purchase inspection will cover and what it won't. Get separate written estimates for findings that could affect your decision; no generic G2 maintenance budget can resolve an unexamined defect.

## Arrange training and insurance before delivery

Send the exact aircraft and panel configuration, your qualifications and intended flying to your prospective insurer. Ask for the requirements and any limitations in writing before committing to a delivery plan. A seller's statement about training doesn't bind an insurer.

[Cirrus Embark's current terms](https://embark.cirrusaircraft.com/) describe complimentary transition or differences training for qualifying pre-owned owners, with application and completion deadlines. They exclude using the complimentary instruction for a flight review, instrument proficiency check or ferry flight. Check your eligibility and arrange the training provider directly; the program isn't an automatic substitute for every delivery or insurance requirement.

Tell the instructor which navigator, autopilot and display combination you're buying. Ask for a plan that addresses normal operation, mode awareness and the applicable abnormal and emergency procedures, including CAPS training. Budget time to learn the airplane you will actually receive.

## Build a purchase decision from the missing answers

[Download the G2 records-comparison checklist](/blog/sr22-g2-prebuy-records-checklist.md) and use one copy per candidate. Separate documented facts, seller statements and unresolved items. Put each prospective expense beside the finding or intended change that creates it.

If you [browse the aircraft classifieds](/aircraft), use the two G2 listings as starting points for those requests, then recheck availability and specifications with the seller. An airplane that already has the equipment you want may reduce planned changes. It still needs a records review, a physical inspection and a mission calculation. Choose with those answers in hand.
