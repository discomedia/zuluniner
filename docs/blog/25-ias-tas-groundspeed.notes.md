## Original calculations and diagram

All three cases are hypothetical ground-study problems, not actual aircraft performance, a current listing's calibration data, recalled FAA exam questions or operating procedures. The first explicitly assumes a fictional IAS-to-CAS correction. Standard pressure at 6,000 ft pressure altitude and 20°C gives a density ratio of about 0.787709; treating CAS as EAS at the stated low speed gives 114.9257 KTAS, rounded to 115. The article explicitly neglects compressibility for this teaching approximation. Its 20-kt direct-headwind leg gives 95 kt GS and 31.5789 minutes for 50 NM.

The second case uses 100 NM each way, 120 KTAS and 30 kt direct wind: 66.6667 plus 40 minutes, totaling 106.6667 minutes, versus 100 minutes in still air. The third uses 100 KTAS and a 20-kt west-to-east crosswind with a maintained northbound track: 11.53696° left correction, heading 348.46304° true and 97.97959 kt GS. Metric conversions and rounded figures were checked. The full arithmetic report is ignored `data/blog-25-calculations.json`.

A horizontal-to-vertical ratio of 51.031:250 matches the scenario's 20:97.97959 velocity ratio to drawing precision.
