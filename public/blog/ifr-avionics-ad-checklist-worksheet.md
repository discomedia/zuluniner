# IFR avionics ad: seller questions and evidence worksheet

Original buying aid for US Part 91 small-airplane screening. Prepared 21 October 2026. This isn't an airworthiness determination, dispatch checklist, approved maintenance program or operating procedure. Use the actual aircraft documents and qualified shop/instructor evaluation.

## Define the candidate and mission

* Aircraft registration, model, serial number and airworthiness category:
* Intended routes and representative destination/alternate procedures:
* Required approach minima/capabilities, with current procedure references:
* Personal requirements, including any autopilot capability:
* Planned alternative if satellite navigation is unavailable:
* Instructor and shop who will assess the proposed arrangement:

## Request aircraft and installation documents

* Current approved flight-manual material and applicable operating limitations:
* Navigator exact model, installed software and approved functions:
* Applicable approval and AFMS document number, revision and configuration:
* Displays, CDI/HSI, source-selection and annunciation arrangement:
* Autopilot exact model, interfaces, approved modes and limitations:
* Alteration/installation records required for this installation:
* Equipment list, current weight-and-balance records and maintenance instructions:
* Evidence that the documentation matches the installed aircraft:

## Record each inspection separately

* Section 91.411 entry: date, systems covered, tested altitude, record reference:
* Section 91.413 entry: date, transponder identity, record reference:
* Section 91.171 basis, if IFR use of VOR is intended: procedure/check, date and required record:
* Work since those entries, including static-system opening or altitude-reporting/transponder maintenance:
* Required subsequent tests and evidence of completion, as determined by the maintainer:
* Inoperative equipment, applicable assessment and proposed remedy:

Don't assume an annual entry covers these separate requirements. The test intervals have different scopes; intervening maintenance can trigger further testing.

## Scope a qualified evaluation and checkout

* Functions and records the avionics shop agrees to assess:
* Source changes, approach guidance, autopilot modes and missed-approach behavior to cover with the shop/instructor:
* Power/sensing dependencies and applicable failure procedures to understand:
* Discrepancies and their written resolution:
* Approved modification proposal, cost basis, exclusions and expected downtime:
* Instructor checkout, insurance conditions and pilot qualification/proficiency checks:
